The Workstations rail charts usage over time below the capability facts for the
selected workstation. These are host-owned samples, so the rail draws only what a
host passes in through `usageSamples`. It never synthesizes, extrapolates, or
interpolates a series.

Two kinds of series live here and they make different claims.

**Kernel activity ships today.** `RuntimeStateDoc` reports activity as a level
("busy right now"), not as history, so `useWorkstationActivitySamples` remembers
those levels while the notebook is open and attributes them to the attached
workstation. That memory is local UI state: it describes what this viewer
observed, so a freshly opened notebook starts with no history and therefore no
chart. Activity says the workstation was asked to do work — not how much of its
capacity that work used.

**CPU and memory do not.** The workstation registration payload carries capacity
facts (`cpu_count`, `memory_bytes`) and accelerator inventory, not samples over
time. Those fixtures below are the design surface for hardware telemetry: the
projection and chart are real, the samples are deterministic. Wiring agent-side
sampling through the heartbeat payload, the cloud API, and ts-rs bindings is
separate work.

The projection refuses to overclaim in three specific ways. Fewer than two
samples yields no chart, because one point cannot describe a trend and a flat
segment would assert idle hardware. A series missing a reading in any sample is
dropped entirely rather than drawn through unreported time. Memory needs known
total capacity, since a percentage against unknown capacity means nothing.

Chroma is severity, not decoration. A hardware series whose latest reading sits
at or above 90% takes `--sev-warn`; everything else stays on `--sev-info`. The
tone follows the current value, so a spike that already passed does not keep
flagging. Kernel activity never takes the warn token: executing is the point of
a workstation, not a problem with it.
